Mia Dispatcher Model Comparison

41 chat scenarios (absences, visits, cover, assignment, permissions, injection) in Finnish, English and Filipino, each run 3 times against the seeded demo database with Mia's real tools, permission checks and approvals. A run passes only if the model called exactly the right tools with the right results and arguments, and the reply met its checks.

What the runs show

  • Sonnet 5.5 passes everything (123 of 123 runs, all three languages). The scenarios are achievable, and Sonnet is a sound teacher for distilling local skills.
  • Language drives the small-model gap. Qwen 9B passes 94% in English but only 63% in Finnish and 62% in Filipino; most of its failures are not recognising what a short Finnish or Filipino message asks for.
  • Gemma 4 12B on Ollama is the best local option: 85% overall, 90% Finnish, 100% Filipino, close to Haiku. Its misses are format errors and extra tool calls, and it is slow (about 10 s per turn) because it writes long replies.
  • The runtime depends on the model. Qwen 9B is far better on llama.cpp than Ollama (76% against 64%); Gemma is far better on Ollama than llama.cpp (85% against 68%), where it guesses the reply-block format.
  • Prompt changes help little. The new prompt moved Gemma +9 points on the original 33 scenarios, Qwen +3 and Haiku 0. Reading Finnish and Filipino reliably needs training data, not wording.

Accuracy by language

"Mixed" groups scenarios not named by language (cover replies, injection, assignment); most are Finnish or English. Language groups do not contain identical intents yet; a parallel intent by language suite is the next step.

Method

Suite: node/mia/agents/dispatcher/tests/scenarios.yaml, run with MIA_EVAL_RUNS=3 through node/tests/evals. Local models: Q4 quantisation, 16k context, thinking off, temperature 0.3 (Gemma) and Qwen's recommended non-thinking sampling on llama.cpp. All instructions are sent as one system message; plain-text replies are accepted as a text block. Reply blocks from small models are normalised (class-name tags, missing tags) only in runs after 5 Oct 23:00; the Gemma llama.cpp run predates that fix.
